Section 01 · Overview

Fifteen stores carry most of the decline, and a costed plan can recover most.

Fat Tuesday runs about 80 frozen-daiquiri stores, mostly in Las Vegas, Louisiana and Florida. This packet sets out the turnaround: where the business stands, why sales fell, which stores are worth saving, the levers inside the stores, a phased recovery plan, what it delivers by 2027 and how progress is tracked each week. It reads Toast point-of-sale data, the company's P&L and store EBITDA, and the outside market together.

The decline sits in fifteen stores, and the rest of the estate has recovered.

Same-store sales are down 9.5% over 52 weeks, and fifteen stores account for most of it. The other 63 stores have recovered to flat on their own. EBITDA fell in 2025 while total revenue held on new openings: rent, labour and marketing explain over half of the drop, and other costs most of the rest.

What holds. 11 of the fifteen still earn strong four-wall margins, so the turnaround is a sales recovery at good stores, not a rescue of broken ones.

What to watch. Whether the fifteen stop falling. They were down 10% in the last four weeks and show no recovery trend of their own yet.

Section 04 · Market context

The market is soft, but it does not explain the fifteen.

Peers, local foot traffic, Las Vegas, New Orleans and Florida indicators, prices and the event calendar, each dated. The market sets the tide; this tab separates it from what the stores can change.

Ark Restaurants, which runs food and drink inside New York-New York on the Strip and in Florida, reports a 6.6% decline and cites lower Strip traffic. Dave & Buster's and RCI's nightclubs are slightly down. Food-led chains with a value message are growing.

What holds. Fat Tuesday's trend improved too, by about two and a half points between the 52-week and 12-week windows.

What to watch. No public same-store benchmark exists for bars or frozen-drink concepts, so every comparison here is approximate.

What holds. Glendale, Bourbon, Project 63 and Neonopolis track their blocks almost exactly. For them, the plan is about the area, not the store.

What to watch. Tempe's block grew 25% while its checks fell 15%. More people walk past; fewer come in.
